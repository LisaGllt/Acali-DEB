12  Discussion & Conclusions

12.1 Single compound effect

The DEB-TKTD analysis confirmed the marked difference in toxicity between imidacloprid and epoxiconazole observed in Aporrectodea caliginosa, for which reproduction EC\(_{50}\) values were previously estimated at 0.55 and 126.8 mg\(\cdot\)kg\(^{-1}\) for imidacloprid and epoxiconazole, respectively (Gollot et al., 2026). The internal tolerance concentrations was estimated at 1016 [682 ; 1431] ng\(\cdot\)g^{-1} for imidacloprid, compared with 43,637 [11,232 ; 88,458] ng\(\cdot\)g\(^{-1}\) for epoxiconazole, indicating that physiological stress was initiated at a substantially lower internal exposure for imidacloprid. This difference is consistent with the litterature with reported reproduction EC\(_{50}\) values of 0.21-1.89 mg\(\cdot\)kg\(^{-1}\) in Eisenia species, depending on soil properties and experimental conditions (Bandeira et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2019).

At toxicokinetic equilibrium, the internal tolerance concentrations can be translated into approximate external soil concentrations using the bioaccumulation factors previously estimated for the two compounds. Based on a bioaccumulation factor of 21, the imidacloprid tolerance concentration corresponds to an external concentration of approximately 0.048 [0.032 ; 0.068] mg\(\cdot\)kg\(^{-1}\). For epoxiconazole, using a bioaccumulation factor of 0.9, the corresponding external concentration is approximately 48.5 [12.5 ; 98.3] mg\(\cdot\)kg\(^{-1}\). These external-equivalent thresholds are consistent with the concentration ranges over which effects were observed experimentally and further illustrate the much greater potency of imidacloprid. However, these conversions should be interpreted cautiously because they assume constant exposure and toxicokinetic equilibrium.

12.2 Physiological modes of action

The physiological modes of action selected for the two pesticides both involved a reduction in assimilation. For imidacloprid, the model combining effects on assimilation and growth received the strongest support, whereas an effect on assimilation alone was sufficient to describe epoxiconazole toxicity. The recurrent support for reduced assimilation suggests that a decrease in the energy entering the organism represents a plausible common mechanism underlying the observed reductions in growth and reproduction. In DEB terms, reduced assimilation decreases the energy available for all downstream processes, thereby providing a coherent explanation for concurrent effects on multiple life-history traits without requiring a direct effect on reproduction.

This interpretation is biologically plausible for both compounds, although the underlying mechanisms may differ. Imidacloprid is a neuroactive compound that can alter activity, feeding behavior, and burrowing in earthworms, while also affecting detoxification pathways and cellular integrity (Dittbrenner et al., 2010; Kaur et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2019; Zang et al., 2000). A reduction in feeding or food processing could therefore be represented at the DEB level as impaired assimilation. The additional support for a direct effect on growth suggests that reduced energy acquisition alone was not sufficient to reproduce all observed responses. This growth-related effect may represent increased energetic costs associated with tissue production, impaired biosynthesis, or the diversion of resources toward detoxification and cellular repair. However, the DEB-TKTD pMoA should be interpreted as a functional representation of the affected energetic process rather than as direct evidence for a specific biochemical mechanism.

For epoxiconazole, the assimilation-only model was retained because models combining assimilation with growth or reproduction produced only marginal improvements in BIC. Previous studies have shown that epoxiconazole exposure can induce oxidative stress and reduce energetic reserves in earthworms (Givaudan, 2014; Pelosi et al., 2016). Such responses may increase the energetic demands associated with detoxification or decrease feeding and assimilation efficiency. Within the present model, these different biological processes may be summarized by an apparent reduction in assimilated energy. The fact that no additional direct effect on reproduction was required also suggests that reduced cocoon production could be explained indirectly by a lower energy supply rather than by a specific impairment of reproductive allocation.

12.3 Model limitations and perspective of improvement

The selected models generally captured the concentration-dependent responses across juvenile growth, adult growth, and reproduction. The epoxiconazole model reproduced the overall trends without systematic deviations across the exposure range, supporting the adequacy of a relatively simple assimilation-based pMoA. For imidacloprid, however, the residual pattern was more structured, with effects tending to be overestimated at intermediate concentrations and underestimated at the highest concentrations. Several processes may explain this discrepancy. First, the toxicodynamic stress function may be too smooth to reproduce a relatively abrupt transition between weak and severe effects. Other forms of stress functions could be considered, like a Hill function. Second, the toxicokinetics of imidacloprid showed evidence of saturation at high exposure levels, restricting the application domain of the TK model. Internal concentrations, and consequently toxic effects, may therefore be less accurately predicted for the highest treatments. Finally, mortality observed in adults at the highest concentration could not be reproduced because the present model did not include a survival module. The predicted growth and reproduction trajectories at this concentration consequently describe only sublethal processes and do not account for the loss of individuals.

Although the estimated tolerance concentrations provide a mechanistically relevant measure of sensitivity, their credible intervals remained relatively broad, particularly for epoxiconazole. This uncertainty likely results partly from the distribution of tested concentrations. Relatively few treatments were located close to the inferred onset of effects, whereas many observations corresponded either to concentrations producing little response or to concentrations already associated with marked toxicity. Additional experimental treatments concentrated around the estimated tolerance concentrations would improve identification of the threshold and help distinguish more clearly between competing pMoAs. Such data would be particularly valuable for epoxiconazole, for which the uncertainty interval around the tolerance concentration covered a wide range of internal concentrations.

The available experimental data also covered separate and very restricted portions of the life cycle rather than following individuals continuously from hatching to adulthood and reproduction. Endpoints were measured over a short period of time with juvenile growth measured at only three time points, adult growth at two time points and cocoon production at only one time point over 28 days. Combining these endpoints within a single DEB-TKTD framework provides a more integrated analysis than endpoint-specific dose-response models, but it does not fully constrain the temporal propagation of toxic effects across the life cycle. Experiments following individuals from emergence to maturity and reproduction under continuous exposure would provide stronger information on delayed maturation, carry-over effects, and changes in energy allocation between life stages. Finally, effects on embryonic development could not be incorporated into the present model because the available data were insufficient to estimate an embryo-specific damage process. Although cocoon production was quantified, the model did not explicitly represent potential effects on cocoon quality, embryonic development time, hatching success, or hatchling condition. This limitation may be important because reproductive toxicity can arise not only through reduced maternal investment but also through direct exposure of cocoons and embryos. Future DEB-TKTD developments should therefore integrate embryonic development and hatching data, ideally within experiments covering the complete life cycle. Such data would allow discrimination between effects on adult energy allocation, cocoon production, and embryo viability, thereby improving both the mechanistic interpretation and predictive capacity of the model.
