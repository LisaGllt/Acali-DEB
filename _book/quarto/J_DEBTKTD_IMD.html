format_summary <- function(df) {
  df %>%
    rename_with(~ str_remove(., "\\.1\\.$")) %>%
    rownames_to_column("stat") %>%
    pivot_longer(
      cols = -c(stat, Model),

For imidacloprid, comparison of the candidate physiological modes of action showed that the best-supported model combined effects on assimilation and growth (Table 11.1). This formulation had the lowest BIC (BIC = -64.7), followed by the growth and reproduction model (BIC = -60.7; \(\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=4.0\)) and the assimilation and reproduction model (BIC = -57.1; \(\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=7.6\)). The assimilation-only formulation received substantially less support (BIC = -49.7; \(\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=15.0\)). These results provide clear support for the combined assimilation and growth pMoA, while the growth and reproduction formulation remained a plausible, although less supported, alternative. Overall, imidacloprid effects were best represented by impaired energy assimilation combined with a direct effect on growth, rather than by reduced assimilation alone or by effects restricted to maintenance or reproduction.

The selected assimilation and growth model estimated a no-effect concentration (\(c_0\)) of 1,122 [893 ; 1,511] ng\(\cdot\)g\(^{-1}\) (internal concentration) and a stress slope parameter (\(b\)) of 0.014 [0.011 ; 8.1] g\(\cdot\)ng\(^{-1}\). The broader relative uncertainty associated with \(b\) indicates that the increase in stress above the no-effect concentration was less precisely estimated than the threshold itself.

Overall, the selected DEB-TKTD model captured the main concentration-response patterns observed for juvenile growth, adult growth, and reproduction (Figure 11.21 & Figure 11.19). It reproduced the general decline in life-history performance with increasing imidacloprid exposure, supporting a physiological mode of action involving both reduced assimilation and altered growth processes.

Nevertheless, some systematic deviations remained across the exposure gradient. The model overestimated the effect on juvenile growth at intermediate imidacloprid concentrations (0.158 and 0.5 mg\(\cdot\)kg\(^{-1}\)) resulting in predicted values below the observations. Reproduction was similarly underestimated over this intermediate concentration range. For imidacloprid, comparison of the candidate physiological modes of action showed that the best-supported model combined effects on assimilation and growth (@tbl-IMDTKTDres). This formulation had the lowest BIC (BIC = -64.7), followed by the growth and reproduction model (BIC = -60.7; $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=4.0$) and the assimilation and reproduction model (BIC = -57.1; $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=7.6$). The assimilation-only formulation received substantially less support (BIC = -49.7; $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=15.0$). These results provide clear support for the combined assimilation and growth pMoA, while the growth and reproduction formulation remained a plausible, although less supported, alternative. Overall, imidacloprid effects were best represented by impaired energy assimilation combined with a direct effect on growth, rather than by reduced assimilation alone or by effects restricted to maintenance or reproduction.

The selected assimilation and growth model estimated a no-effect concentration ($c_0$) of 1,122 \[893 ; 1,511\] ng$\cdot$g$^{-1}$ (internal concentration) and a stress slope parameter ($b$) of 0.014 \[0.011 ; 8.1\] g$\cdot$ng$^{-1}$. The broader relative uncertainty associated with $b$ indicates that the increase in stress above the no-effect concentration was less precisely estimated than the threshold itself.

Overall, the selected DEB-TKTD model captured the main concentration-response patterns observed for juvenile growth, adult growth, and reproduction (@fig-pred_F_IMD_AGsummary & @fig-predobs_F_IMD_AG). It reproduced the general decline in life-history performance with increasing imidacloprid exposure, supporting a physiological mode of action involving both reduced assimilation and altered growth processes.

Nevertheless, some systematic deviations remained across the exposure gradient. The model overestimated the effect on juvenile growth at intermediate imidacloprid concentrations (0.158 and 0.5 mg$\cdot$kg$^{-1}$) resulting in predicted values below the observations. Reproduction was similarly underestimated over this intermediate concentration range. At the highest concentrations, between 1.6 and 5 mg$\cdot$kg$^{-1}$, the model moderately underestimated the effect on growth and cocoon production.
