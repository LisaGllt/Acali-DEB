format_summary <- function(df) {
  df %>%
    rename_with(~ str_remove(., "\\.1\\.$")) %>%
    rownames_to_column("stat") %>%
    pivot_longer(
      cols = -c(stat, Model),
      names_to = "param",
      values_to = "value"
    ) %>%
    pivot_wider(
      names_from = stat,
      values_from = value
    ) %>%
    mutate(
      value = str_c(
        round(Result_mode, 4),
        " [", round(`2.5%`, 4),
        " ; ", round(`97.5%`, 4), "]"
      )
    ) %>%
    dplyr::select(Model, param, value) %>%
    pivot_wider(
      names_from = param,
      values_from = value
    )
}

For imidacloprid, comparison of the candidate physiological modes of action showes that models involving reduced assimilation receives the strongest support within the DEB-TKTD framework (Table 11.1). The lowest BIC is obtained for the combined assimilation and growth pMoA (BIC = -49.1), followed by the assimilation-only model (BIC = -42.5). The resulting difference (\(\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=6.6\)) provides clear support for the combined formulation, although the assimilation-only model remained the second-best candidate. In contrast, models affecting maintenance, reproduction, or growth alone are less supported. Overall, these results indicate that imidacloprid effects are best represented by impaired energy assimilation combined with a direct effect on growth processes, rather than by a direct alteration of maintenance or reproductive allocation.

The selected assimilation and growth model estimates a tolerance concentration (\(z\)) of 1016 ng g\(^{-1}\) (internal concentration), with a 95% credible interval of 682-1431 ng\(\cdot\)g\(^{-1}\). The stress slope parameter (\(b\)) is estimated at \(9.0 \times 10^{-4}\) g\(\cdot\)ng\(^{-1}\), with a 95% credible interval of \(5.0 \times 10^{-4}\) to \(2.6 \times 10^{-3}\) g\(\cdot\)ng\(^{-1}\). The broader relative uncertainty associated with \(b\) indicates that the increase in stress above the tolerance concentration is less precisely estimated than the threshold itself.

Overall, the selected DEB-TKTD model captures the main concentration-response patterns observed for juvenile growth, adult growth, and reproduction (Figure 11.22). It reproduces the general decline in life-history performance with increasing imidacloprid exposure, supporting a physiological mode of action involving both reduced assimilation and altered growth processes.

Nevertheless, systematic deviations remaines across the exposure gradient. For both juvenile and adult growth, the model tends to overestimate imidacloprid effects at intermediate external concentrations, approximately between 0.158 and 0.5 mg\(\cdot\)kg\(^{-1}\), resulting in predicted growth values below the observations. Conversely, the model underestimates effects at the highest concentrations, particularly above 0.89 mg\(\cdot\)kg\(^{-1}\), and consequently predicts higher growth than observed. A similar pattern is observed for reproduction, with effects being overestimated at intermediate concentrations and underestimated at the highest concentrations.

format_summary <- function(df) {
  df %>%
    rename_with(~ str_remove(., "\\.1\\.$")) %>%
    rownames_to_column("stat") %>%
    pivot_longer(
      cols = -c(stat, Model),
      names_to = "param",
      values_to = "value"
    ) %>%
    pivot_wider(
      names_from = stat,
      values_from = value
    ) %>%
    mutate(
      value = str_c(
        round(Result_mode, 4),
        " [", round(`2.5%`, 4),
        " ; ", round(`97.5%`, 4), "]"
      )
    ) %>%
    dplyr::select(Model, param, value) %>%
    pivot_wider(
      names_from = param,
      values_from = value
    )
}

For imidacloprid, comparison of the candidate physiological modes of action showes that models involving reduced assimilation receives the strongest support within the DEB-TKTD framework (@tbl-IMDTKTDres). The lowest BIC is obtained for the combined assimilation and growth pMoA (BIC = -49.1), followed by the assimilation-only model (BIC = -42.5). The resulting difference ($\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=6.6$) provides clear support for the combined formulation, although the assimilation-only model remained the second-best candidate. In contrast, models affecting maintenance, reproduction, or growth alone are less supported. Overall, these results indicate that imidacloprid effects are best represented by impaired energy assimilation combined with a direct effect on growth processes, rather than by a direct alteration of maintenance or reproductive allocation.

The selected assimilation and growth model estimates a tolerance concentration ($z$) of 1016 ng g$^{-1}$ (internal concentration), with a 95% credible interval of 682-1431 ng$\cdot$g$^{-1}$. The stress slope parameter ($b$) is estimated at $9.0 \times 10^{-4}$ g$\cdot$ng$^{-1}$, with a 95% credible interval of $5.0 \times 10^{-4}$ to $2.6 \times 10^{-3}$ g$\cdot$ng$^{-1}$. The broader relative uncertainty associated with $b$ indicates that the increase in stress above the tolerance concentration is less precisely estimated than the threshold itself.

Overall, the selected DEB-TKTD model captures the main concentration-response patterns observed for juvenile growth, adult growth, and reproduction (@fig-pred_F_IMD_AGsummary). It reproduces the general decline in life-history performance with increasing imidacloprid exposure, supporting a physiological mode of action involving both reduced assimilation and altered growth processes.

Nevertheless, systematic deviations remaines across the exposure gradient. For both juvenile and adult growth, the model tends to overestimate imidacloprid effects at intermediate external concentrations, approximately between 0.158 and 0.5 mg$\cdot$kg$^{-1}$, resulting in predicted growth values below the observations. Conversely, the model underestimates effects at the highest concentrations, particularly above 0.89 mg$\cdot$kg$^{-1}$, and consequently predicts higher growth than observed. A similar pattern is observed for reproduction, with effects being overestimated at intermediate concentrations and underestimated at the highest concentrations.
