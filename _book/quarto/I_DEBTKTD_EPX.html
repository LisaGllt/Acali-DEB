format_summary <- function(df) {
  df %>%
    rename_with(~ str_remove(., "\\.1\\.$")) %>%
    rownames_to_column("stat") %>%
    pivot_longer(
      cols = -c(stat, Model),
      names_to = "param",
      values_to = "value"
    ) %>%
    pivot_wider(
      names_from = stat,
      values_from = value
    ) %>%
    mutate(
      value = if_else(
        param == "b",
        str_c(
          formatC(Result_mode, format = "e", digits = 2),
          " [", formatC(`2.5%`, format = "e", digits = 2),
          " ; ", formatC(`97.5%`, format = "e", digits = 2), "]"
        ),
        str_c(
          round(Result_mode, 3),
          " [", round(`2.5%`, 3),
          " ; ", round(`97.5%`, 3), "]"
        )
      )
    ) %>%
    dplyr::select(Model, param, value) %>%
    pivot_wider(
      names_from = param,
      values_from = value
    )
}

For epoxiconazole, comparison of the candidate physiological modes of action did not clearly discriminate among the three best-supported formulations (Table 10.1). The lowest BIC was obtained for the assimilation and reproduction pMoA (BIC = -84.5), closely followed by the growth and reproduction model (BIC = -84.4; \(\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=0.1\)) and the assimilation and growth model (BIC = -83.7; \(\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=0.8\)). These three formulations therefore received essentially equivalent support from the data. The assimilation-only model also remained plausible (BIC = -83.3; \(\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=1.2\)), whereas models combining maintenance with reproduction or assimilation received less support, with BIC values of -81.5 and -80.7, respectively. Although the assimilation and reproduction formulation had the lowest BIC and was retained for subsequent analyses, its advantage over the other best-performing models was marginal. The available data therefore support several alternative representations of epoxiconazole effects and do not allow a clear distinction between direct effects on assimilation, growth, and reproduction.

The selected assimilation and reproduction model estimated a no-effect concentration (\(c_0\)) of 47,992 [15,105; 100,090] ng\(\cdot\)g\(^{-1}\) internal concentration and a stress slope parameter (\(b\)) of \(2.4 \times 10^{-6}\) [\(9.7 \times 10^{-7}\) ; \(5.5 \times 10^{-6}\)] g\(\cdot\)ng\(^{-1}\). The broad credible interval around \(c_0\) indicates substantial uncertainty in the internal concentration at which toxic stress begins. Although \(b\) was comparatively better constrained, its credible interval also indicates uncertainty in the subsequent increase in stress above the no-effect concentration.

For epoxiconazole, comparison of the candidate physiological modes of action did not clearly discriminate among the three best-supported formulations (@tbl-ResEPX). The lowest BIC was obtained for the assimilation and reproduction pMoA (BIC = -84.5), closely followed by the growth and reproduction model (BIC = -84.4; $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=0.1$) and the assimilation and growth model (BIC = -83.7; $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=0.8$). These three formulations therefore received essentially equivalent support from the data. The assimilation-only model also remained plausible (BIC = -83.3; $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=1.2$), whereas models combining maintenance with reproduction or assimilation received less support, with BIC values of -81.5 and -80.7, respectively. Although the assimilation and reproduction formulation had the lowest BIC and was retained for subsequent analyses, its advantage over the other best-performing models was marginal. The available data therefore support several alternative representations of epoxiconazole effects and do not allow a clear distinction between direct effects on assimilation, growth, and reproduction.

The selected assimilation and reproduction model estimated a no-effect concentration ($c_0$) of 47,992 \[15,105; 100,090\] ng$\cdot$g$^{-1}$ internal concentration and a stress slope parameter ($b$) of $2.4 \times 10^{-6}$ \[$9.7 \times 10^{-7}$ ; $5.5 \times 10^{-6}$\] g$\cdot$ng$^{-1}$. The broad credible interval around $c_0$ indicates substantial uncertainty in the internal concentration at which toxic stress begins. Although $b$ was comparatively better constrained, its credible interval also indicates uncertainty in the subsequent increase in stress above the no-effect concentration.

Overall, the selected DEB-TKTD model reproduced the concentration-dependent responses observed for juvenile growth, adult growth, and reproduction (@fig-pred_G_EPX_ARsummary & @fig-predobs_G_EPX_AR). The model captured the general magnitude and shape of the decline in life-history performance with increasing epoxiconazole exposure. However, some systematic deviations remained. Growth effects were underestimated at the lowest tested concentrations, between 0.928 and 9.28 mg$\cdot$kg$^{-1}$, resulting in predicted growth values above the observations. Reproductive effects were also underestimated at the highest concentrations, between 43.1 and 200 mg$\cdot$kg$^{-1}$, with predicted cocoon production exceeding the observed values. Despite these discrepancies, the combined assimilation and reproduction pMoA provided an overall satisfactory description of epoxiconazole effects across the three endpoints.
