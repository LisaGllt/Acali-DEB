5.1 Classication DEB standard formulation

Reserve energy dynamic

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm d t} = \frac{\{\dot p_{Am}\}_t}{L} \ \left(f - \frac{E\ \nu_t}{\{\dot p_{Am}\}_t}\right) \]

With :

  • \(E\) : Energy in the reserve (\(J\))
  • \(\nu_t\) : Energy conductance at temperature \(T_{exp}\) (\(cm.d^{-1}\))

Growth

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\mathrm d L}{\mathrm d t} &= \frac{\nu_t}{3\ (\frac{E}{E_m} + g)} \left(\frac{E}{E_m} - \frac{L}{L_m}\right) \\ \\ K_m &= \frac{[\dot p_{M,t}]}{[E_G]} \\ E_m &= \frac{\{\dot p_{Am}\}_t}{\nu_t}\\ g &= \frac{[E_G]}{\kappa_{\text{real}}\ E_m}\\ L_m &= \frac{\nu_t}{K_m\ g} \end{aligned} \]

With :

  • \(L\) : Structural volumetric length (\(cm\))
  • \([\dot p_{M,t}]\) : Volume-specific maintenance rate (\(J.d^{-1}.cm^{-3}\))
  • \([E_G]\) : Volume-specific cost for structure (\(J.cm^{-3}\))

\[ W_W = L^3 \left(1+\frac{E}{E_m}w\right) \]

With :

  • \(W_W\) : Wet weight of the earthworm (\(g\))
  • \(w\) : Contribution of reserve to body weight (\(g.cm^{-3}\))

Maturity and reproduction

\[ \dot p_C = \frac{g\ E}{g + \frac{E}{E_m}} (\nu_t\ L^2 + K_m\ L^3) \]

With :

  • \(\dot p_C\) : Mobilized energy flux from reserve (\(J.d^{-1}\))

\[ \dot p_R = (1-\kappa_{\text{real}})\ \dot p_C - \dot k_{J,t}\ E_h \]

With :

  • \(\dot p_R\) : Allocated energy flux to reproduction (\(J.d^{-1}\))
  • \(\dot k_{J,t}\) : Maturity maintenance rate (\(d^{-1}\))

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}E_H}{\mathrm{d}t}= \begin{cases} E_H \leq E_H^p,\ & \dot{p}_R\\ E_H > E_H^p,\ & 0 \end{cases} \]

With :

  • \(E_h\) : Maturity (\(J\))
  • \(E_h^p\) : Maturity at puberty (\(J\))

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\mathrm{d}t}= \begin{cases} E_H \leq E_H^p,\ & 0 \\ E_H > E_H^p,\ & \kappa_R\ \frac{\dot{p}_R}{E_{0}} \end{cases} \]

With :

  • \(R\) : Cumulated reproduction (#)
  • \(\kappa_R\) : Reproduction efficiency (-)
  • \(E_0\) : Energy cost for an egg (\(J\))

Effect of temperature on rates

$$

\[\begin{aligned} s_A &= \exp(\frac{T_A}{T_{ref}-\frac{T_A}{T_{exp}+273.15}})\\ s_{rH} &= \frac{1 + \exp (\frac{T_A^H}{T^H}-\frac{T_A^H}{T_{ref}+ 273.15})}{1 + \exp (\frac{T_A^H}{T^H}-\frac{T_A^H}{T_{exp}+273.15})}\\ T_c &= s_A (T_{exp}+273.15 \geq T_{ref}) s_{rH} + (T_{exp} + 273.15 < T_{ref})\\ \\ \{\dot p_{Am}\}_t &= \{\dot p_{Am}\}\ T_c\\ [\dot p_{M,t}] &= [\dot p_{M}]\ T_c \\ \nu_t &= \nu \ T_c \\ \dot k_{J,t} &= \dot k_{J} \ T_c \end{aligned}\]

$$

5.2 Energy assimilation in the case of earthworms

\[ \dot Q_{f,max} = \frac{\{\dot p_{Am}\}_t\ L^2}{\kappa_X} \]

With :

  • \(\dot Q_{f,max}\) : Maximal rate of energy ingested by the earthworm (\(J.d^{-1}\))
  • \(\{\dot p_{Am}\}_t\) : Surface-area-specific maximum assimilation rate at temperature \(T_{exp}\) (\(J.d^{-1}.cm^{-2}\))
  • \(L\) : Structural volumetric length (\(cm\))
  • \(\kappa_X\) : Fraction of food energy fixed in reserve (digestion efficiency) (-)

\[ \begin{aligned} \dot Q_{f,soil} &= [\dot{F}_m]\ L^2 \times (\mu_{OM}\ r_{OM}^{clx, horse}\ \frac{OM_{soil}}{Nb_{ind}}) \\ \dot Q_{f,horse} &= [\dot{F}_m]\ L^2 \times (\mu_{OM}\ \frac{OM_{horse}}{Nb_{ind}}) \\ \dot Q_{f,tot} &= \dot Q_{f,horse} + \dot Q_{f,soil} \end{aligned} \]

With :

  • \(\dot Q_{f,soil}\) : Rate of energy ingested from the organic matter in the soil by the earthworm (\(J.d^{-1}\))
  • \(\dot Q_{f,horse}\) : Rate of energy ingested from the organic matter in the horse dung by the earthworm (\(J.d^{-1}\))
  • \(\dot Q_{f,tot}\) : Rate of energy ingested from organic matter by the earthworm (\(J.d^{-1}\))
  • \([\dot{F}_m]\) : Maximum volume-specific searching rate (\(g.d^{-1}.cm^{-3}\))
  • \(W_w\) : Wet weight of the earthworm (\(g\))
  • \(\mu_{OM}\) : Horse dung organic matter energy content (\(J.g^{-1}\))
  • \(r_{OM}^{clx, horse}\) : Ratio between soil organic matter energy content and horse dung organic matter energy content (-)
  • \(OM_{soil}\) : Organic matter from soil in a cosm (g)
  • \(OM_{horse}\) : Organic matter from horse dung in a cosm (g)
  • \(Nb_{ind}\) : Number of individual in the cosm (#)

\[ \begin{aligned} \dot{OM}_{out}^{soil} &= \frac{\dot Q_{f,soil}}{\mu_{OM}\ r_{OM}^{clx, horse}}\ Nb_{ind} \\ \dot{OM}_{out}^{horse} &= \frac{\dot Q_{f,horse}}{\mu_{OM}} \ Nb_{ind} \end{aligned} \]

With :

  • \(\dot{OM}_{out}^{soil}\) : Rate of consumption of soil organic matter by all earthworms of the cosm (\(g.d^{-1}\))
  • \(\dot{OM}_{out}^{horse}\) : Rate of consumption of horse dung organic matter by all earthworms of the cosm (\(g.d^{-1}\))

\[ f=\frac{X}{X+1}, \ \ \text{with }\ X = \frac{\dot Q_{f,tot}}{\frac 1 2 \dot Q_{f,max}} \]

With :

  • \(f\) : Functional response to organic matter (-)

5.3 Different model versions tested

5.3.1 Relationship of wet weight to structural length

In the standard DEB model, reserve is proportional to structure under constant food condition. As it is not the case in the different experiment of this study (especially bart2019_XP3), we considered another equation where reserve is independent to structure.

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Model w:}\\ &W_w = L^3\left(d_V + \frac{E}{E_m} w_E\right) \ \text{(DEB-std)}\\ \\ \text{Model e:}\\ &W_w = L^3\ d_V + E\ d_E \end{aligned} \]

5.3.2 Soil ingestion

Ingestion is often considered to be proportional to the structural surface of the individual (L\(^2\), model L) but we tested the possibility of it being proportional to structural volume and therefore to weight (L\(^3\), model W).

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Model L:}\\ \dot Q_{f,soil} &= [\dot{F}_m]\ L^2 \times (\mu_{OM}\ r_{OM}^{clx, horse}\ \frac{OM_{soil}}{Nb_{ind}}) \\ \dot Q_{f,horse} &= [\dot{F}_m]\ L^2 \times (\mu_{OM}\ \frac{OM_{horse}}{Nb_{ind}}) \\ \\ \text{Model W:}\\ \dot Q_{f,soil} &= [\dot{F}_m]\ L^3 \times (\mu_{OM}\ r_{OM}^{clx, horse}\ \frac{OM_{soil}}{Nb_{ind}}) \\ \dot Q_{f,horse} &= [\dot{F}_m]\ L^3 \times (\mu_{OM}\ \frac{OM_{horse}}{Nb_{ind}}) \end{aligned} \]

5.3.3 Functional response to organic matter

Hyperbolic function (Model Y)

\[ f=\frac{X}{X+1}, \ \ \text{with }\ X = \frac{\dot Q_{f,tot}}{\frac 1 2 \dot Q_{f,max}} \]

Normalized hyperbolic function reaching 1 at maximum ingestion (Model K)

\[ f=\frac{X}{K(1-X)+X}, \ \ \text{with }\ X = \frac{\dot Q_{f,tot}}{\dot Q_{f,max}} \]

Code 
K <- 0.006
x_hy <- seq(0,1,0.01)
x_hi <- seq(0,1,0.01)

f_hyperbolic <- function(x){
  res <- 2*x/(1+2*x)
  return(res)
}

f_hill <- function(x, Slope){
  res <- x/(K*(1-x) + x)
  return(res)
}

y_hy <- f_hyperbolic(x_hy)
y_hi <- f_hill(x_hi, Slope)

df <- data.frame(
  x = c(x_hy, x_hi),
  y = c(y_hy, y_hi),
  Type = c(rep("Hyperbolic", length(x_hy)), rep("Normalized hyperbolic", length(x_hi)))
)

p <- ggplot(
  data = df,
  aes(
    x=x,
    y=y,
    color = Type
  )
)+
  geom_line()+
  labs(x = "Qf/Qfmax", y = "f")+
  scale_color_manual(values = c(Nord_frost[2], Nord_frost[4]))+
  theme_minimal()
p

5.3.4 Change in kappa

The standard DEB model supposes a fixed \(\kappa\) throughout the organism life (model F). But because of the observations of a switch in growth when earthworms are adult and able to reproduce with another individual, we tested a switch in energy allocation when earthworms are adults and not alone in a cosm, and therefore able to reproduce (model S).

\[ \begin{aligned} {\text{Model F: }}\\ &\kappa_{\text{real}} = \kappa \\ \\ {\text{Model C: }}\\ & x = \begin{cases} E_h < E_h^p, & 0 \\ E_h \geq E_h^p, &\min \left(1,\frac{t-t_p}{\tau}\right) \end{cases}\\ &\kappa_{A,real} = (1-x)\ \kappa_J + x\ \kappa_A \\ &\kappa_{real} = \begin{cases} E_h < E_h^p, & \kappa_J & (\text{Juveniles})\\ E_h \geq E_h^p\ \&\ Nb_{ind} = 1, & \kappa_J & (\text{Adult alone})\\ E_h \geq E_h^p\ \&\ Nb_{ind} > 1, & \kappa_{A,real} & (\text{Grouped adults}) \end{cases} \end{aligned} \]

# Model formulation

## Classication DEB standard formulation

**Reserve energy dynamic**

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm d t} = \frac{\{\dot p_{Am}\}_t}{L} \ \left(f - \frac{E\ \nu_t}{\{\dot p_{Am}\}_t}\right)
$$

With :

-   $E$ : Energy in the reserve ($J$)
-   $\nu_t$ : Energy conductance at temperature $T_{exp}$ ($cm.d^{-1}$)

**Growth**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm d L}{\mathrm d t} &= \frac{\nu_t}{3\ (\frac{E}{E_m} + g)} \left(\frac{E}{E_m} - \frac{L}{L_m}\right) \\ \\
K_m &= \frac{[\dot p_{M,t}]}{[E_G]} \\
E_m &= \frac{\{\dot p_{Am}\}_t}{\nu_t}\\
g &= \frac{[E_G]}{\kappa_{\text{real}}\ E_m}\\
L_m &= \frac{\nu_t}{K_m\ g}
\end{aligned}
$$

With :

-   $L$ : Structural volumetric length ($cm$)
-   $[\dot p_{M,t}]$ : Volume-specific maintenance rate ($J.d^{-1}.cm^{-3}$)
-   $[E_G]$ : Volume-specific cost for structure ($J.cm^{-3}$)

$$
W_W = L^3 \left(1+\frac{E}{E_m}w\right)
$$

With :

-   $W_W$ : Wet weight of the earthworm ($g$)
-   $w$ : Contribution of reserve to body weight ($g.cm^{-3}$)

**Maturity and reproduction**

$$
\dot p_C = \frac{g\ E}{g + \frac{E}{E_m}} (\nu_t\ L^2 + K_m\ L^3)
$$

With :

-   $\dot p_C$ : Mobilized energy flux from reserve ($J.d^{-1}$)

$$
\dot p_R = (1-\kappa_{\text{real}})\ \dot p_C - \dot k_{J,t}\ E_h
$$

With :

-   $\dot p_R$ : Allocated energy flux to reproduction ($J.d^{-1}$)
-   $\dot k_{J,t}$ : Maturity maintenance rate ($d^{-1}$)

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}E_H}{\mathrm{d}t}= 
\begin{cases}
E_H \leq E_H^p,\ & \dot{p}_R\\
E_H > E_H^p,\ & 0
\end{cases}
$$

With :

-   $E_h$ : Maturity ($J$)
-   $E_h^p$ : Maturity at puberty ($J$)

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\mathrm{d}t}= 
\begin{cases}
E_H \leq E_H^p,\ & 0 \\
E_H > E_H^p,\ & \kappa_R\ \frac{\dot{p}_R}{E_{0}}
\end{cases}
$$

With :

-   $R$ : Cumulated reproduction (#)
-   $\kappa_R$ : Reproduction efficiency (-)
-   $E_0$ : Energy cost for an egg ($J$)

**Effect of temperature on rates**

\$\$

\begin{aligned}
s_A &= \exp(\frac{T_A}{T_{ref}-\frac{T_A}{T_{exp}+273.15}})\\
s_{rH} &= \frac{1 + \exp (\frac{T_A^H}{T^H}-\frac{T_A^H}{T_{ref}+ 273.15})}{1 + \exp (\frac{T_A^H}{T^H}-\frac{T_A^H}{T_{exp}+273.15})}\\
T_c &= s_A (T_{exp}+273.15 \geq T_{ref}) s_{rH} + (T_{exp} + 273.15 < T_{ref})\\ \\

\{\dot p_{Am}\}_t &= \{\dot p_{Am}\}\ T_c\\
[\dot p_{M,t}] &= [\dot p_{M}]\ T_c \\
\nu_t &= \nu \ T_c \\
\dot k_{J,t} &= \dot k_{J} \ T_c
\end{aligned}

\$\$

## Energy assimilation in the case of earthworms

$$
\dot Q_{f,max} = \frac{\{\dot p_{Am}\}_t\ L^2}{\kappa_X}
$$

With :

-   $\dot Q_{f,max}$ : Maximal rate of energy ingested by the earthworm ($J.d^{-1}$)
-   $\{\dot p_{Am}\}_t$ : Surface-area-specific maximum assimilation rate at temperature $T_{exp}$ ($J.d^{-1}.cm^{-2}$)
-   $L$ : Structural volumetric length ($cm$)
-   $\kappa_X$ : Fraction of food energy fixed in reserve (digestion efficiency) (-)

\begin{aligned}
\dot Q_{f,soil} &= [\dot{F}_m]\ L^2 \times (\mu_{OM}\ r_{OM}^{clx, horse}\ \frac{OM_{soil}}{Nb_{ind}}) \\
\dot Q_{f,horse} &= [\dot{F}_m]\ L^2 \times (\mu_{OM}\ \frac{OM_{horse}}{Nb_{ind}}) \\
\dot Q_{f,tot} &= \dot Q_{f,horse} + \dot Q_{f,soil}
\end{aligned}
$$

With :

-   $\dot Q_{f,soil}$ : Rate of energy ingested from the organic matter in the soil by the earthworm ($J.d^{-1}$)
-   $\dot Q_{f,horse}$ : Rate of energy ingested from the organic matter in the horse dung by the earthworm ($J.d^{-1}$)
-   $\dot Q_{f,tot}$ : Rate of energy ingested from organic matter by the earthworm ($J.d^{-1}$)
-   $[\dot{F}_m]$ : Maximum volume-specific searching rate ($g.d^{-1}.cm^{-3}$)
-   $W_w$ : Wet weight of the earthworm ($g$)
-   $\mu_{OM}$ : Horse dung organic matter energy content ($J.g^{-1}$)
-   $r_{OM}^{clx, horse}$ : Ratio between soil organic matter energy content and horse dung organic matter energy content (-)
-   $OM_{soil}$ : Organic matter from soil in a cosm (g)
-   $OM_{horse}$ : Organic matter from horse dung in a cosm (g)
-   $Nb_{ind}$ : Number of individual in the cosm (#)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot{OM}_{out}^{soil} &= \frac{\dot Q_{f,soil}}{\mu_{OM}\ r_{OM}^{clx, horse}}\ Nb_{ind} \\
\dot{OM}_{out}^{horse} &= \frac{\dot Q_{f,horse}}{\mu_{OM}} \ Nb_{ind}
\end{aligned}
$$

With :

-   $\dot{OM}_{out}^{soil}$ : Rate of consumption of soil organic matter by all earthworms of the cosm ($g.d^{-1}$)
-   $\dot{OM}_{out}^{horse}$ : Rate of consumption of horse dung organic matter by all earthworms of the cosm ($g.d^{-1}$)

$$
f=\frac{X}{X+1}, \ \ \text{with }\ X = \frac{\dot Q_{f,tot}}{\frac 1 2 \dot Q_{f,max}} 
$$

With :

-   $f$ : Functional response to organic matter (-)

## Different model versions tested

### Relationship of wet weight to structural length

In the standard DEB model, reserve is proportional to structure under constant food condition. As it is not the case in the different experiment of this study (especially bart2019_XP3), we considered another equation where reserve is independent to structure.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Model w:}\\
&W_w = L^3\left(d_V + \frac{E}{E_m} w_E\right) \ \text{(DEB-std)}\\ \\
\text{Model e:}\\
&W_w = L^3\ d_V + E\ d_E
\end{aligned}
$$

### Soil ingestion

Ingestion is often considered to be proportional to the structural surface of the individual (L$^2$, model L) but we tested the possibility of it being proportional to structural volume and therefore to weight (L$^3$, model W).

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Model L:}\\
\dot Q_{f,soil} &= [\dot{F}_m]\ L^2 \times (\mu_{OM}\ r_{OM}^{clx, horse}\ \frac{OM_{soil}}{Nb_{ind}}) \\
\dot Q_{f,horse} &= [\dot{F}_m]\ L^2 \times (\mu_{OM}\ \frac{OM_{horse}}{Nb_{ind}}) \\ \\
\text{Model W:}\\
\dot Q_{f,soil} &= [\dot{F}_m]\ L^3 \times (\mu_{OM}\ r_{OM}^{clx, horse}\ \frac{OM_{soil}}{Nb_{ind}}) \\
\dot Q_{f,horse} &= [\dot{F}_m]\ L^3 \times (\mu_{OM}\ \frac{OM_{horse}}{Nb_{ind}})
\end{aligned}
$$

### Functional response to organic matter

**Hyperbolic function (Model Y)**

$$
f=\frac{X}{X+1}, \ \ \text{with }\ X = \frac{\dot Q_{f,tot}}{\frac 1 2 \dot Q_{f,max}} 
$$

**Normalized hyperbolic function reaching 1 at maximum ingestion (Model K)**

$$
f=\frac{X}{K(1-X)+X}, \ \ \text{with }\ X = \frac{\dot Q_{f,tot}}{\dot Q_{f,max}} 
$$

### Change in kappa

The standard DEB model supposes a fixed $\kappa$ throughout the organism life (model F). But because of the observations of a switch in growth when earthworms are adult and able to reproduce with another individual, we tested a switch in energy allocation when earthworms are adults and not alone in a cosm, and therefore able to reproduce (model S).

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\text{Model F: }}\\ &\kappa_{\text{real}} = \kappa \\ \\
{\text{Model C: }}\\ 
& x = 
\begin{cases}
E_h < E_h^p, & 0 \\
E_h \geq E_h^p, &\min \left(1,\frac{t-t_p}{\tau}\right)
\end{cases}\\
&\kappa_{A,real} = (1-x)\ \kappa_J + x\ \kappa_A \\
&\kappa_{real} = 
\begin{cases}
E_h < E_h^p, & \kappa_J  & (\text{Juveniles})\\
E_h \geq E_h^p\ \&\ Nb_{ind} = 1, & \kappa_J & (\text{Adult alone})\\
E_h \geq E_h^p\ \&\ Nb_{ind} > 1, & \kappa_{A,real} & (\text{Grouped adults})
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
