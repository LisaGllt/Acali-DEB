7  Case of isolated adults

  • Show All Code
  • Hide All Code
  • View Source

The case of isolated adults was therefore explored only through simulations to investigate the resulting patterns of energy allocation in the absence of reproductive opportunities. Two alternative formulations were considered: (i) maintaining the juvenile allocation fraction throughout adulthood (\(\kappa_J\)), and (ii) introducing a transition toward the adult allocation fraction (\(\kappa_A\)) after maturation, as implemented for grouped adults.

The formulation maintaining the juvenile allocation fraction throughout adulthood (\(\kappa_J\)) reproduced the observed growth trajectories of isolated adults more accurately than the formulation including a transition toward the adult allocation fraction (\(\kappa_A\)), which systematically underestimated growth after maturation (Figure 7.1). This result suggests that isolated adults maintain growth patterns closer to those of juveniles than to those of grouped reproducing adults.

However, despite the absence of observed cocoon production in isolated adults, both formulations still predicted a non-zero allocation of energy toward reproduction after maturation (Figure 7.2). This discrepancy indicates that the energetic fate of the reproductive flux remains inadequately represented in the current model formulations and must be further investigated.

The formulation maintaining the juvenile allocation fraction throughout adulthood ($\kappa_J$) reproduced the observed growth trajectories of isolated adults more accurately than the formulation including a transition toward the adult allocation fraction ($\kappa_A$), which systematically underestimated growth after maturation (@fig-predkapW). This result suggests that isolated adults maintain growth patterns closer to those of juveniles than to those of grouped reproducing adults.

However, despite the absence of observed cocoon production in isolated adults, both formulations still predicted a non-zero allocation of energy toward reproduction after maturation (@fig-predkapR). This discrepancy indicates that the energetic fate of the reproductive flux remains inadequately represented in the current model formulations and must be further investigated.
