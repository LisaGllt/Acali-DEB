format_summary <- function(df) {
  df %>%
    rename_with(~ str_remove(., "\\.1\\.$")) %>%
    rownames_to_column("stat") %>%
    pivot_longer(
      cols = -c(stat, Model),
      names_to = "param",
      values_to = "value"
    ) %>%
    pivot_wider(
      names_from = stat,
      values_from = value
    ) %>%
    mutate(
      value = if_else(
        param == "b",
        str_c(
          formatC(Result_mode, format = "e", digits = 2),
          " [", formatC(`2.5%`, format = "e", digits = 2),
          " ; ", formatC(`97.5%`, format = "e", digits = 2), "]"
        ),
        str_c(
          round(Result_mode, 3),
          " [", round(`2.5%`, 3),
          " ; ", round(`97.5%`, 3), "]"
        )
      )
    ) %>%
    dplyr::select(Model, param, value) %>%
    pivot_wider(
      names_from = param,
      values_from = value
    )
}

For epoxiconazole, the lowest BIC was obtained for the model combining effects on growth and reproduction (BIC = -86.1; Table 10.1). However, the assimilation-and-reproduction formulation received virtually equivalent support (BIC = -85.9; \(\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=0.2\)). The assimilation and maintenance model also remained plausible (BIC = -84.2; \(\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=1.9\)), whereas the assimilation and growth formulation was less supported (BIC = -83.4; \(\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=2.7\)). The assimilation-only and maintenance-and-growth models received substantially lower support, with \(\Delta\mathrm{BIC}\) values above 5. Overall, the available data did not allow a clear statistical distinction between the growth-and-reproduction and assimilation-and-reproduction pMoAs. Because epoxiconazole exposure has been associated with altered energy reserves and impaired reproductive performance (ECHA, 2025; Givaudan, 2014; Kjærstad et al., 2010; Pelosi et al., 2016; Taxvig et al., 2007; Zarn et al., 2003), the assimilation-and-reproduction formulation was retained as the most biologically plausible representation, while acknowledging the growth-and-reproduction formulation as an equally plausible statistical alternative.

The selected assimilation-and-reproduction model estimated a no-effect concentration (\(c_0\)) of 47,101 [14,367; 96,352] ng\(\cdot\)g\(^{-1}\) internal concentration and a stress slope parameter (\(b\)) of \(2.2 \cdot 10^{-6}\) [\(0.9 \cdot 10^{-6}\); \(4.7 \cdot 10^{-6}\)] g\(\cdot\)ng\(^{-1}\). The broad credible interval around \(c_0\) indicates substantial uncertainty in the internal concentration at which toxic stress begins, whereas the stress slope was comparatively better constrained.

  df %>%

    pivot_longer(
      cols = -c(stat, Model),

        )
      )

For epoxiconazole, the lowest BIC was obtained for the model combining effects on growth and reproduction (BIC = -86.1; @tbl-ResEPX). However, the assimilation-and-reproduction formulation received virtually equivalent support (BIC = -85.9; $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=0.2$). The assimilation and maintenance model also remained plausible (BIC = -84.2; $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=1.9$), whereas the assimilation and growth formulation was less supported (BIC = -83.4; $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=2.7$). The assimilation-only and maintenance-and-growth models received substantially lower support, with $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}$ values above 5. Overall, the available data did not allow a clear statistical distinction between the growth-and-reproduction and assimilation-and-reproduction pMoAs. Because epoxiconazole exposure has been associated with altered energy reserves and impaired reproductive performance [@givaudan_2014; @pelosi_2016; @zarn_2003; @taxvig_2007; @kjaerstad_2010; @echa_2025], the assimilation-and-reproduction formulation was retained as the most biologically plausible representation, while acknowledging the growth-and-reproduction formulation as an equally plausible statistical alternative.

The selected assimilation-and-reproduction model estimated a no-effect concentration ($c_0$) of 47,101 \[14,367; 96,352\] ng$\cdot$g$^{-1}$ internal concentration and a stress slope parameter ($b$) of $2.2 \cdot 10^{-6}$ \[$0.9 \cdot 10^{-6}$; $4.7 \cdot 10^{-6}$\] g$\cdot$ng$^{-1}$. The broad credible interval around $c_0$ indicates substantial uncertainty in the internal concentration at which toxic stress begins, whereas the stress slope was comparatively better constrained.

Predicted internal epoxiconazole concentrations increased approximately proportionally with external exposure concentration (@fig-pred_G_EPX_AR_summary_Ciarticle, @tbl-Cirecap). Maximum internal concentrations were generally reached within the first 4-6 days of exposure, considerably earlier than for imidacloprid, and were slightly higher in adults than in juveniles. The estimated no-effect concentration of 47,101 ng$\cdot$g$^{-1}$ was exceeded only at the two highest exposure concentrations, 92.8 and 200 mg$\cdot$kg$^{-1}$. At 20.0 mg$\cdot$kg$^{-1}$ and 43.1 mg$\cdot$kg$^{-1}$, the predicted maximum internal concentration was below the median estimate of the no-effect threshold. However, given the wide credible interval around $c_0$, this exposure condition may still exceed the threshold for part of the posterior distribution.
