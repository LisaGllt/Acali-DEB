format_summary <- function(df) {
  df %>%
    rename_with(~ str_remove(., "\\.1\\.$")) %>%
    rownames_to_column("stat") %>%
    pivot_longer(
      cols = -c(stat, Model),
      names_to = "param",
      values_to = "value"
    ) %>%
    pivot_wider(
      names_from = stat,
      values_from = value
    ) %>%
    mutate(
      value = if_else(
        param == "b",
        str_c(
          formatC(Result_mode, format = "e", digits = 2),
          " [", formatC(`2.5%`, format = "e", digits = 2),
          " ; ", formatC(`97.5%`, format = "e", digits = 2), "]"
        ),
        str_c(
          round(Result_mode, 3),
          " [", round(`2.5%`, 3),
          " ; ", round(`97.5%`, 3), "]"
        )
      )
    ) %>%
    dplyr::select(Model, param, value) %>%
    pivot_wider(
      names_from = param,
      values_from = value
    )
}

For epoxiconazole, comparison of the candidate physiological modes of action shows that the three best-supported models all involve reduced assimilation (Table 10.1 ). The lowest BIC is obtained for the combined assimilation and growth pMoA (BIC = -93.3), followed closely by the assimilation and reproduction model (BIC = -92.6) and the assimilation-only model (BIC = -92.4). The corresponding BIC differences are small (\(\Delta\mathrm{BIC}<1\)), indicating that the three formulations receive essentially equivalent support from the data. Because the addition of direct effects on growth or reproduction do not substantially improve model performance, the assimilation-only formulation is retained as the most parsimonious model.

The selected assimilation model estimated a tolerance concentration (\(z\)) of 43,637. ng g(^{-1}) internal concentration, with a 95% credible interval of 11,231-88,457 ng\(\cdot\)g\(^{-1}\). The stress slope parameter (\(b\)) was estimated at \(2.68 \times 10^{-6}\) ng\(\cdot\)g\(^{-1}\), with a 95% credible interval of \(1.56 \times 10^{-6}\) to \(4.89 \times 10^{-6}\) g\(\cdot\)ng\(^{-1}\). The wide credible interval around \(z\) indicates substantial uncertainty in the internal concentration at which toxic stress begins, whereas the stress slope was estimated more precisely.

Overall, the selected DEB-TKTD model accurately reproduces the concentration-dependent responses observed for juvenile growth, adult growth, and reproduction (Figure 10.21). No systematic pattern is apparent in the deviations between observed and predicted responses across the exposure gradient, with no consistent overestimation or underestimation of effects at low, intermediate, or high concentrations. These results indicate that an impairment of assimilation alone is sufficient to describe the observed effects of epoxiconazole across all three endpoints.

format_summary <- function(df) {
  df %>%
    rename_with(~ str_remove(., "\\.1\\.$")) %>%
    rownames_to_column("stat") %>%
    pivot_longer(
      cols = -c(stat, Model),
      names_to = "param",
      values_to = "value"
    ) %>%
    pivot_wider(
      names_from = stat,
      values_from = value
    ) %>%
    mutate(
      value = if_else(
        param == "b",
        str_c(
          formatC(Result_mode, format = "e", digits = 2),
          " [", formatC(`2.5%`, format = "e", digits = 2),
          " ; ", formatC(`97.5%`, format = "e", digits = 2), "]"
        ),
        str_c(
          round(Result_mode, 3),
          " [", round(`2.5%`, 3),
          " ; ", round(`97.5%`, 3), "]"
        )
      )
    ) %>%
    dplyr::select(Model, param, value) %>%
    pivot_wider(
      names_from = param,
      values_from = value
    )
}

For epoxiconazole, comparison of the candidate physiological modes of action shows that the three best-supported models all involve reduced assimilation (@tbl-ResEPX ). The lowest BIC is obtained for the combined assimilation and growth pMoA (BIC = -93.3), followed closely by the assimilation and reproduction model (BIC = -92.6) and the assimilation-only model (BIC = -92.4). The corresponding BIC differences are small ($\Delta\mathrm{BIC}<1$), indicating that the three formulations receive essentially equivalent support from the data. Because the addition of direct effects on growth or reproduction do not substantially improve model performance, the assimilation-only formulation is retained as the most parsimonious model.

The selected assimilation model estimated a tolerance concentration ($z$) of 43,637. ng g(\^{-1}) internal concentration, with a 95% credible interval of 11,231-88,457 ng$\cdot$g$^{-1}$. The stress slope parameter ($b$) was estimated at $2.68 \times 10^{-6}$ ng$\cdot$g$^{-1}$, with a 95% credible interval of $1.56 \times 10^{-6}$ to $4.89 \times 10^{-6}$ g$\cdot$ng$^{-1}$. The wide credible interval around $z$ indicates substantial uncertainty in the internal concentration at which toxic stress begins, whereas the stress slope was estimated more precisely.

Overall, the selected DEB-TKTD model accurately reproduces the concentration-dependent responses observed for juvenile growth, adult growth, and reproduction (@fig-pred_A_EPX_Asummary). The model captures both the magnitude and general shape of the decline in life-history performance with increasing epoxiconazole exposure. No systematic pattern is apparent in the deviations between observed and predicted responses across the exposure gradient, with no consistent overestimation or underestimation of effects at low, intermediate, or high concentrations. These results indicate that an impairment of assimilation alone is sufficient to describe the observed effects of epoxiconazole across all three endpoints.
